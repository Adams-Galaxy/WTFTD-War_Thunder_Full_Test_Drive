# Code of conduct

WTFTD is made by and for War Thunder players, from many countries and in many languages. To keep its issues,
discussions and pull requests a good place to be, everyone who takes part agrees to this code.

**In short: be kind, stay on topic, and assume good faith.**

## What we expect

- Respect for everyone, whatever their experience, age, nationality, language, gender, orientation, religion,
  disability, appearance or anything else. English isn't everyone's first language: be patient with it.
- Criticism of ideas, code and choices, never of people. Say what's wrong and, if you can, how to make it better.
- Help for newcomers: everyone started with their first bug report.
- Accepting that the maintainer has the last word on what goes into the project, and that "no" or "not now" can happen.

## What isn't accepted

- Insults, slurs, personal attacks, harassment, threats, or picking on someone again and again.
- Discrimination or hateful content, in words, images or user names.
- Sexual content or violent images.
- Posting someone's private information (name, address, accounts, files) without their clear permission.
- Trolling, spam, advertising, and flooding threads off topic.
- Asking for or sharing ways to get an unfair advantage in online battles, or to get round the game's rules.
  WTFTD is for testing in your own missions and for overlays from your own vehicle's data: requests to show
  enemies or to change the game online will be closed.

## Where it applies

In this repository: issues, pull requests, discussions, comments, commit messages, and the wiki if there is one.
Also wherever someone speaks for the project in public.

## Reporting

If someone breaks this code, or you're not sure, tell us:

- report the comment, issue or discussion with GitHub's **⋯ → Report content**, and choose to report it to the
  repository's admins (or to GitHub itself for anything serious);
- for a security problem, follow [SECURITY.md](SECURITY.md) instead.

Reports are read by the maintainer and kept private. You won't be blamed for a report made in good faith.

## What can happen

Depending on what happened, the maintainer may: ask for a change, edit or hide a comment, lock or close a thread,
give a warning, or block someone from the repository for a while or for good. Anything illegal is passed on to
GitHub.
